NEW BATAVIA: RECONNECT
======================

Design document, synced with the build on 2026-08-29.

This file was originally a pitch draft. It is now two things at once, and
the split is marked throughout:

    BUILT       running code, playable in the browser today
    WRITTEN     script and reference material that exists, but has no
                playable loop behind it yet
    DESIGNED    intent only. No code, no script.

Where the old draft and the build disagreed, the build won and the draft
was corrected. The eras were renumbered along the way: what the pitch
called eight eras is nine in the build, and the pitch's own
"era-specific conflicts" list was off by one against its own
progression list. Both are fixed below.


STATUS AT A GLANCE
==================

BUILT
-----

- Era 1, the first voice. A complete day loop with its own win condition.

- Glossary: 42 technology terms, most with a drawn diagram and a real

WRITTEN

PREMISE
=======

Human civilization has collapsed. Modern communications infrastructure
is gone: internet, cellular networks, data centres, satellites, power
grids, long-distance telecommunications.

Humanity has been reduced to isolated settlements.

The protagonist is a telecommunications graduate who became a
programmer. He never worked in telecommunications, but university left
him with an understanding of networking, communications, electronics,
and the architecture underneath the Internet.

After surviving the collapse in Jakarta, he concludes:

    "We don't need to rebuild the old world first.
     We need to reconnect the people who survived."

He establishes the RECONNECT project.

The ultimate objective:

    Rebuild a functional Internet-like network across Indonesia.

It begins with people, scraps, batteries and crude AM radios, and ends
with computers, packet switching, routing, and a TCP/IP-like
internetworking system.

It takes 38 years, and the man who understood it best is not alive at
the end of them.


SETTING
=======

Post-apocalyptic Indonesia. The starting region is Jakarta, renamed by
survivors:

    NEW BATAVIA

BUILT — the region as it actually exists in the code. 13 settlements,
each with a real longitude, latitude and mast height:

    New Batavia (HQ)     Tanjung Priok        Kemayoran
    Menteng               Senayan              Kebayoran
    Cawang                Pulo Gadung          Bekasi
    Tangerang             Serang               Depok
    Bogor

Six salvage sites sit among them, each a named piece of the old world:

    Gambir Exchange       telephone exchange
    Muara Karang Station  power station
    Manggarai Depot       railway depot
    Jatinegara Market     electronics market
    Cakung Warehouses     warehouse belt
    Cilincing Scrapyards  scrapyard

DESIGNED — the expansion the script talks about and the map does not
show: Bandung, Cirebon, Yogyakarta, Semarang, Surabaya, then Sumatra,
Bali, Kalimantan, Sulawesi.

The map is the primary interface. It is isometric, rendered in Pixi over
a baked terrain, with settlements, roads, rivers, terrain, salvage
sites, camps, masts, links and moving couriers drawn on it. React mounts
it once and then stays out of the way.


THE NINE ERAS
=============

Each era has a title, a year, a premise the menu shows, and an objective.
Eras 0 and 1 are playable. The rest are readable.

ERA 0 — HUMANITY REBUILDS                                  2030   BUILT
    Premise:   40 villages, and none of them can hear each other.
    Objective: Walk the region on foot, find out who is still alive, and
               count what is left in the store.
    Tech:      Couriers, notice boards, signal fires, line of sight.

ERA 1 — THE FIRST VOICE                                    2031   BUILT
    Premise:   100 kilometres, and a radio that has to answer twice.
    Objective: Raise a radio tower, a generator and a transmitter above
               New Batavia, and hold the link to Tangerang through a
               rainy season.
    Tech:      AM, transmitter, antenna, generator, battery bank,
               propagation and the skip zone.

ERA 2 — THE COPPER WAR                                   2034   WRITTEN
    Premise:   The network is on the ground now, where anyone can reach
               it.
    Objective: String 400 kilometres of telegraph across the Jakarta
               region, and keep it standing while people cut it down.
    Conflict:  Copper is currency. The line is a bank with no door on it.
               It is not defended by guarding it; it is defended by
               giving every village it passes an hour on it, so the wire
               becomes theirs.

ERA 3 — WHO OWNS THE LINE                                2039   WRITTEN
    Premise:   Everyone depends on it. Nobody voted for it.
    Objective: Run switchboards for 9 settlements, and negotiate who owns
               each exchange as fast as you can build them.
    Conflict:  The project decides who may speak to whom, at what price,
               in what order. That is a government. Nobody elected it.
               The answer is to hand the exchanges away and keep only the
               standard.

ERA 4 — MAKE, NOT FIND                                   2045   WRITTEN
    Premise:   The old world runs out of parts before it runs out of
               problems.
    Objective: Build the glassworks and the chemistry under it, and make
               valves that outlast the ones you can no longer find.
    Conflict:  Salvage ends. The project stops being scavengers and
               becomes manufacturers, which needs a chemical industry it
               does not have.

ERA 5 — THE COUNTING ROOM                                2048   WRITTEN
    Premise:   It cannot remember anything, and it must never be switched
               off.
    Objective: Build a machine out of valves that does the arithmetic the
               region runs on, and keep 3,000 of them alight through a
               rainy season.
    Event:     Pak Min dies. See below.

ERA 6 — THE THINKING MACHINE                             2052   WRITTEN
    Premise:   Memory you can hold in your hand, and the first person who
               never saw the old world.
    Objective: Thread core memory, build a machine that can hold a
               routing table, and teach the generation born after the
               war.
    Conflict:  Dedicated circuits between every pair of cities do not
               scale. Messages become packets and nodes start deciding
               for themselves.

ERA 7 — THE DRAWER RUNS OUT                              2061   WRITTEN
    Premise:   Every router in the country runs on a chip that nobody
               alive can make.
    Objective: Stretch the last salvaged chips across 15 provinces, while
               9 of them argue about a furnace at Cilegon.
    Conflict:  Salvaged silicon is a thousand times better than anything
               the workshop can build, and there is a fixed amount of it
               left in the world. Every recovery is smaller than the
               last.

ERA 8 — A PATH NOBODY CHOSE                              2068   WRITTEN
    Premise:   The last thing to build is the thing that makes you
               unnecessary.
    Objective: Run the fab until it works, publish everything you
               learned, and hand the network to people who never asked
               your permission.
    Ending:    A message from New Batavia to Surabaya along a route
               nobody specified. Bayu tries to reconstruct the path and
               cannot. That is the win condition.


ERA 0 — THE SURVEY (BUILT)
==========================

The day loop before there is anything to switch on. Four phases, the
same deterministic seeding Era 1 uses, and nothing else in common with
it: no hardware, no power, no circuits, no dusk window.

The scarce thing is not copper. It is legs. A message moves only while
somebody is carrying it, so distance is the entire cost model and the
only thing the crew can spend is days.

Crew: Bayu, Mel, Tajuddin. Pak Min has not been found yet.

The one resource is rations. A settlement that will talk to you shares
food, so contact pays for the walking that wins more contact, and a
region that stays strangers starves the project slowly.

What the crew can do with a day:

- Walk somewhere. Roads, not straight lines.
- Make their case at a gate. This opens the parley.
- Nail a notice board to a gate, so a message left there waits 2 days for
  the next courier going the right way instead of for the right one.
- Lay a signal fire on the high ground above a settlement. Two fires that
  can see each other carry one bit, 20 kilometres, instantly. That bit
  means only "a runner is coming".
- Send the letter. A village courier carries it. Once it leaves, it is
  out of the crew's hands, which is the era's whole emotional argument.

WIN CONDITION
    8 settlements in contact, held for 7 consecutive days, with
    Tangerang among them.

THE GATE EXCHANGE (Parley.tsx)
    One line saying what this village needs, four things the crew could
    offer, one click. Deliberately the smallest screen in the era,
    because the player reaches it in the middle of planning a day and
    has to finish it in a glance.

    An earlier version asked the player to decode two clues and spend
    three offers against a running score. That is a game to sit down
    with, not a stop on the way through a day, so it was cut.


ERA 1 — THE FIRST VOICE (BUILT)
===============================

One turn is one day, in four phases that always run in the same order:

    ASSIGN -> RESOLVE -> DUSK BROADCAST -> NIGHT

The scarce thing is the four people. Every raid answered is a salvage
run not made, and every circuit that goes down takes its settlements'
population out of the evening's audience, which is where the rations
come from.

That feedback loop is the game: connect people, and the network pays for
the next piece of itself.

Resources:

    copper    steel    cells    parts    fuel    rations

Kits the workshop can build:

    Radio tower kit    2 days    6 steel
    Transmitter set    3 days    6 copper, 3 parts
    Battery bank       2 days    4 cells, 2 copper
    Generator set
    Feeder

Threats: raids on lit sites, weather, crew injury, and a rainy season
that has to be survived rather than beaten.

THE TUNING BENCH (Tuning.tsx)
    One slider, one meter, one picture, one button. Drag until the meter
    climbs, stop on the peak, lock it in.

    The picture is drawn in units of reach rather than kilometres, which
    is what makes one strip true for every neighbour whatever its mast
    height. It is the only place in the game where the skip zone is a
    hole you can watch a settlement fall into.


THE PIECES THAT STAND ALONE
===========================

Two eras that are not playable have a working piece of their argument
built anyway, reachable from the main menu.

THE ROUTING WALK-THROUGH (Routing.tsx)              Era 6's argument
    One message, one network, and every decision it takes on the way,
    slowed down until it can be watched: the frame going on, the table
    being read, the bits crossing the line, the frame coming off at the
    far end.

    The second thing it shows is the price. The same delivery is modelled
    on every processor the workbench offers, and the cost of thinking is
    measured from that machine's own adder rather than asserted. With a
    chip out of the drawer, the line is the bottleneck and thinking is
    free. With the machine the project can actually build, thinking costs
    about as much as talking.

THE PROCESSOR SIMULATOR (Simulator.tsx)             Era 7's argument
    Three processors drive the same shift registers, one clock at a time:

        MC14500B      a real 1-bit chip, Motorola, 1977
        MOVE          what is left when you delete everything that
                      needs a factory
        Intel 8051    what was in the drawer before the drawer ran out

    A button labelled "clock" advances the machine by one instruction. On
    the 1-bit machines that is also one clock edge; on the 8051 an
    instruction is 1 or 2 machine cycles, which is why the time on screen
    comes from cycles and not from clicks.

THE ERA RELAY CARD (EraRelay.tsx)
    Every chapter opens with it. Serang wants to tell Bogor something.
    Nine places, eight legs, one number at the end.

    In 2030 that number is nine days. By the last era it is
    milliseconds, and nothing about the geography changed — only what is
    standing on it.

    It is deliberately the same errand every time, because a comparison
    where the task also changes proves nothing.


THE GLOSSARY
============

42 technology terms, spread across the nine eras:

    Era 0: 5    Era 1: 6    Era 2: 5    Era 3: 5    Era 4: 5
    Era 5: 2    Era 6: 4    Era 7: 5    Era 8: 5

Each term has: what it is, why it mattered, a real historical citation
with names and dates, and for most of them a numbered diagram whose pins
are captioned step by step.

Terms light up wherever they are said — in the story, in Era 0, in Era 1
— and clicking one opens the panel over whatever screen the player is
on. Indonesian text lights the same terms through its own aliases, so a
term that highlights in one language highlights in the other.


THE SCRIPT
==========

Nine chapters. Each has a premise, an objective, an opening scene and a
closing scene. The whole thing is readable from the main menu without
playing anything.

16 optional skits sit alongside it: 7 in Era 0, 9 in Era 1. A skit
becomes available when the run reaches the state it belongs to, a chip
appears in the top bar, and it waits there until the player asks for it.
Viewing one retires it for that run. None of them ends in an
instruction, because the day loop is the instruction.

LANGUAGES
---------
English and Indonesian, switchable at any time, remembered across
reloads.

The English files own the structure — which era, who speaks, where the
scenes break — and the Indonesian files supply text at matching indices.
A translation can therefore never drift out of step with the scene it
belongs to; the worst that can happen is a missing line, which falls
back to English.

The two languages are not the same register:

- The narrator speaks written Indonesian.
- The four founders speak Jakarta slang to each other — gua, lu, and no
  ceremony, elder or not. Distance shows up as clipped names, not as
  pronouns.
- Everyone who joined later says saya and addresses the founders by
  title. Neither slang nor literary: ordinary polite speech.
- Numbers of 2 and above are written as digits in both languages,
  because people say "3 hari", not "tiga hari".

Indonesian is not a word-for-word translation and cannot be. A contrast
has to name its second term; abstract noun predicates become verbs;
sentence-final particles carry stance; transitive verbs keep objects
English drops. The full rule set is documented at the top of
src/skits.id.ts.


VERIFICATION
============

Six headless checks, because most of what can go wrong here is not
visible in a browser:

    npm run check:script    Both scripts line up, nobody spells a number,
                            every glossary term is said somewhere, every
                            diagram pin has words, and nobody speaks out
                            of register.
    npm run check:relay     The relay time falls every era.
    npm run check:onebit    Every adder over every input pair, on every
                            simulated processor.
    npm run check:routing   The routing model, with no React in the way.
    npm run check:routes    The road network.
    npm run balance:survey  Era 0's economy.


LORE
====

THE FALL
--------

The war began in 2027. It did not end civilization; it started the
cascade.

It took the things hardest to replace and easiest to reach: satellites,
data centres, undersea cables, refineries, ports. Everything after that
failed on its own.

Power grids failed because nobody could deliver fuel. Supply chains
collapsed because nobody could route them. Telecommunications stopped
because the switching was gone and the people who understood it had
scattered. Governments lost the ability to coordinate, which meant they
lost the ability to govern.

The fighting was over in 18 months. Fewer than 200,000 died in it, which
for a war is a small number. The collapse took another 6 years, and
almost nobody noticed it happening, because it happened one unreplaced
part at a time.

A truck ran out of fuel, so the rice never arrived, so a city went
hungry. The same city had no water, because a power station 400
kilometres away needed one small part, and the factory that made that
part was gone.

Jakarta survived as an isolated collection of settlements. The survivors
began calling it NEW BATAVIA.

THE ARGUMENT
------------

Most survivors think rebuilding communication is pointless. Food is
scarce. Bandits control roads. Why spend anything on talking?

    "If we can communicate, we can coordinate."
    "If we can coordinate, we can trade."
    "If we can trade, we can specialize."
    "If we can specialize, we can rebuild."

Era 0 puts the counter-argument in Mel's mouth and does not resolve it:
people are hungry today, not in 8 seconds. The reply is that Depok has
rice and no salt, New Batavia has salt and no rice, and neither knows
about the other — so it is not a talking problem that becomes a food
problem later. It is one problem, and it is the food one.

The 8 seconds is the spine of the whole script. It is how long a phone
call took to connect before the war. It comes back in Era 0's skits, in
Era 1's, and in the relay card that times the same errand nine times.


CHARACTERS
==========

THE FOUNDERS — playable, present for the whole game
---------------------------------------------------

BAYU — Architect
    Telecommunications graduate who became a programmer. Holds the whole
    topology in his head. Surveys the ground, then builds on it.
    Joins: Era 0. Without him nothing starts.

MEL — Quartermaster
    Container driver out of Tanjung Priok, 6 years. Moves copper, cells
    and crews between sites. Knows which roads are open and which are
    watched.
    Joins: Era 0. Without her nothing moves.

TAJUDDIN — Warden
    Guards the lines and the yards. Stands between the copper and the
    raiders.
    Joins: Era 0 in the build, where he walks the survey with Bayu and
    Mel. Without him the copper walks off.

PAK MIN — Wireman
    31 years at the Gambir exchange. Builds and tunes the transmitters;
    if it carries a signal, he soldered it. The harsh mentor.
    Joins: Era 1. Without him no transmitter works twice.
    Dies: Era 5, of being old, in a workshop that works.

The founders are the emotional through-line and they are what the skits
are made of. What changes is what they are: labour in the metro eras,
programs at island scale, chairs and founders at archipelago scale. By
Era 8 the four of them command nothing directly. That is the arc, not a
limitation.

THE REST — script only, not playable
------------------------------------

These eight speak in chapters 2 through 8. They are not party members,
because there is no era loop to recruit them into yet.

IWAN — Machinist
    Motorcycle mechanic. Engines, generators, pole hardware,
    improvisation. Era 2.

SARI — Lineman
    Climbs, strings, splices. Era 2.

AYU — Switchman
    The first operator Bayu ever trained. Runs the exchanges and trains
    the people who run the rest. Era 3.

IBU RATNA — Envoy
    Speaks for the settlements the network now runs through. Era 3.

HENDRA — Glassblower
    Era 4. Without him there are no vacuum tubes, ever.

DEWI — Chemist
    Batteries, acids, solvents, and much later dopants. Era 4.

FAJAR — Coder
    Born after the fall. The first person on the project who never saw
    the thing they are rebuilding. Everyone else is working from memory;
    he is working from description. That makes him the only one who can
    design without nostalgia, and the only one who cannot be argued with
    using "that is not how it used to work". Era 5.

ANISA — Mathematician
    Error correction, addressing, routing. Era 6.

ERA 8 RECRUITS NOBODY
    The last people Bayu meets are not recruits. They are counterparts:
    network founders in Surabaya, Medan, Makassar, running networks he
    does not administer and cannot order. The roster stops growing
    because the work has stopped being his.


THE MENTOR
==========

Pak Min is not an antagonist. His philosophy:

    "A machine that works for five minutes isn't working."

    BAYU:    "The transmitter works."
    PAK MIN: "Today."

He forces the question of reliability, redundancy, maintenance and
scale. Bayu believes civilization must be rebuilt quickly; Pak Min
believes it must be rebuilt correctly. Their philosophies converge.

His arc: engineering over people, then technology exists because of
people, then both. Ending at:

    "Build it so everyone can use it.
     Then build it so it survives them."

PAK MIN DIES IN ERA 5
---------------------

Not to a raid. Not to a fall from a mast. He is old, the project has run
for 18 years, and one morning he does not come to the bench.

What matters is what he knew when he died. He knew, in detail, how a
semiconductor is made. He could describe the furnace, the crystal, every
page of the process, read decades before the war. He never saw one made.

Not because the knowledge was lost — it was in his hands the whole time
— but because knowing how a thing is done and being able to do it are
separated by a chemical industry, a supply chain, and a generation of
people who have to be trained by someone.

    Knowledge survives a collapse easily.
    Capability does not.
    Rebuilding capability is the work, and the work takes a lifetime.

This is the correction to the fantasy the genre usually sells. Nobody
reads a book and produces a transistor.

His last instruction to Fajar is not a technique:

    "Write it down properly. With the date."

That is the same thing he says in Era 1, about a link that answered
twice.


THE FAB
=======

The project tries to manufacture its own semiconductors. It fails, and
each failure names something the civilization did not know it was
missing: silicon purity, crystal growth, mask making, dopant handling,
particulate control.

None of these are workshop problems. Each one needs a chemical industry.
A chemical industry needs a supply chain. A supply chain needs
coordination between regions that have never worked together.

The fab is not a machine to be built. It is the final proof that the
network works.

Yield is the number the player watches:

    0 dies in 400
    1 die in 400
    3 dies in 400

Against it, salvage falls. The crossing point is the end of the game.

WHAT THE FAB IS FOR
-------------------

Not speed. Its first product is a 1-bit machine that anything pulled out
of a dead appliance would embarrass.

Internetworking needs routers at every network boundary, owned by people
the project does not control. Fifteen provinces cannot be supplied out
of a drawer. A network running on rationed scrap belongs to whoever
holds the rations. A network that can be sold chips is a peer.

The fab is how the project stops being necessary. It is also the answer
to the question Era 3 asked:

    "Who owns communication?"
    "Not us. Not any more."


DESIGNED, NOT BUILT
===================

Everything below this line exists in this document and nowhere else.

FACTIONS
--------

THE RECONNECT MOVEMENT
    Build an open, resilient communication network. Communication is
    basic infrastructure.

THE SCRAP RAIDERS
    Immediate survival. "Tomorrow doesn't matter if we don't survive
    today." They dismantle infrastructure for resources.

THE INDUSTRIAL LEAGUE
    Powerful settlements and factories. Infrastructure belongs to
    whoever builds it, and settlements should pay for access.

THE CENTRAL AUTHORITY
    A growing government. A unified country requires a unified
    communication system, centrally controlled.

THE FREE NETWORK MOVEMENT
    Communication should be open to everyone. Allies of the project, and
    a problem, for the same reason.

Note: Era 2's raiders and Era 3's Ibu Ratna are the only places any of
this reaches the script, and neither is written as a faction.

THE ARCHIPELAGO
---------------

The build stops at the Jakarta region. Expansion to Bandung, Cirebon,
Yogyakarta, Semarang, Surabaya, then Sumatra, Bali, Kalimantan and
Sulawesi is described in the script and drawn nowhere.

The visual progression this implies:

    Ruins -> survivor camps -> settlements -> industrial towns ->
    connected cities -> technological civilization

NETWORK PROPERTIES
------------------

The pitch specified that every link would carry range, bandwidth,
reliability, power consumption, construction cost, maintenance cost,
latency, security and physical vulnerability.

The build models range, reliability, power, construction cost and
physical vulnerability. Bandwidth, latency and security are argued in
the script and in the routing walk-through rather than simulated.


THEMES
======

    Technology vs survival
    Centralization vs decentralization
    Knowledge vs control
    Short-term survival vs long-term civilization
    Infrastructure vs politics
    Efficiency vs resilience
    Individual expertise vs collective knowledge
    Knowing how vs being able to

The last one is the spine. The player should constantly experience:

    "We need X."
    "Where do we get X?"
    "Nobody knows how to make X any more."
    "Find someone who knows."

Bayu is not a genius who knows everything. He is the person who
understands that this has to be figured out. The civilization is rebuilt
by hundreds of specialists.


FINAL SCENE
===========

New Batavia. The final packet is sent.

    Destination: SURABAYA
    Source:      NEW BATAVIA

The route is not chosen. The network chooses it. The packet travels
through several independent networks.

    MESSAGE RECEIVED

The team watches. Silence.

    BAYU:    "It's back."
    PAK MIN: "No."

Bayu looks confused.

    "We didn't bring it back."

He smiles.

    "We built a new one."

CUT TO: a map of Indonesia. Thousands of nodes illuminate across the
islands.

    NEW BATAVIA

Note: Pak Min dies in Era 5 and this scene is Era 8. The line is his,
said by someone else, or the scene is a memory. This is unresolved in
the script and needs a decision.
