GAME IDEA DRAFT
================

Working Title:
NEW BATAVIA: RECONNECT

Genre:
- Browser-based management / strategy
- Isometric 2.5D
- Post-apocalyptic civilization rebuilding
- Technology progression
- Infrastructure / network management
- Light exploration and survival
- Character-driven progression

Core Premise:
-------------
Human civilization has collapsed.

Modern communications infrastructure is gone:
- Internet
- Cellular networks
- Data centers
- Satellites
- Power grids
- Long-distance telecommunications

Humanity has been reduced to isolated settlements.

The protagonist is a telecommunications graduate who eventually became a
programmer. Although they never worked directly in telecommunications,
their university knowledge gave them an understanding of networking,
communications, electronics, and the fundamental architecture behind the
Internet.

After surviving the apocalypse in Jakarta, the protagonist realizes:

    "We don't need to rebuild the old world first.
     We need to reconnect the people who survived."

They establish the RECONNECT project.

The ultimate objective:

    Rebuild a functional Internet-like network across Indonesia.

The journey begins with people, scraps, batteries, and crude AM radios,
and eventually reaches computers, packet switching, routing, and a
TCP/IP-like internetworking system.


SETTING
=======

Post-apocalyptic Indonesia.

The starting region is Jakarta, renamed by survivors as:

    NEW BATAVIA

Jakarta is initially surrounded by isolated settlements.

The player gradually establishes communication with:

    Jakarta
       |
    Tangerang
       |
    Serang

and later expands toward:

    Bogor
    Bandung
    Cirebon
    Yogyakarta
    Semarang
    Surabaya

Eventually:

    Sumatra
    Bali
    Kalimantan
    Sulawesi
    Other Indonesian islands

The map itself is the primary gameplay interface.

The player can see:
- Settlements
- Roads
- Rivers
- Mountains
- Ruins
- Factories
- Power infrastructure
- Communication towers
- Telegraph lines
- Telephone lines
- Relay stations
- Radio towers
- Data centers
- Network links

As civilization progresses, the map physically transforms from scattered
survivor settlements into an interconnected technological civilization.


VISUAL STYLE
============

Isometric / 2.5D.

The world should look like a miniature model of post-apocalyptic Indonesia.

Buildings and infrastructure have visible height.

Important infrastructure should physically stand above the map:

    Radio tower
         |
         |
       /---\
      /     \
     /       \

Telegraph poles should form visible lines between settlements.

Large communication towers should be recognizable from the regional map.

The visual progression is important:

    Ruins
      ↓
    Survivor camps
      ↓
    Settlements
      ↓
    Industrial towns
      ↓
    Connected cities
      ↓
    Technological civilization

THE CENTRAL GAMEPLAY IDEA
=========================

The network itself is the main strategic resource.

Every communication link has:

- Range
- Bandwidth
- Reliability
- Power consumption
- Construction cost
- Maintenance cost
- Latency
- Security
- Physical vulnerability

The player must design a network rather than simply construct isolated
buildings.

Example:

    Jakarta -------- Tangerang -------- Serang
       |
       |
     Bogor
       |
     Bandung

If Tangerang's relay station is destroyed:

    Jakarta X Tangerang X Serang

The network becomes fragmented.

Later the player can build redundancy:

    Jakarta -------- Tangerang -------- Serang
       |                 |                |
       |                 |                |
       +------ Bogor ----+---- Bandung ---+

The player gradually learns concepts such as:

- Redundancy
- Routing
- Fault tolerance
- Network topology
- Bandwidth
- Latency
- Distributed systems

without these necessarily being presented as abstract lessons.


TECHNOLOGICAL PROGRESSION
=========================

ERA 0 — BEFORE TELECOMMUNICATIONS
---------------------------------

Technology:
- Messengers
- Flags
- Signal fires
- Notice boards
- Couriers

Communication is extremely slow.

The player's first objective is simply to establish reliable contact
between nearby settlements.


ERA 1 — AM RADIO
----------------

First major breakthrough:

    AM RADIO

Required:
- Copper
- Wire
- Batteries
- Generator
- Antenna
- Transmitter
- Radio operators

First major achievement:

    NEW BATAVIA → TANGERANG

The player establishes the first long-distance communication link.

Problems:
- Limited range
- Interference
- Power shortages
- Damaged equipment
- Need for trained radio operators


ERA 2 — TELEGRAPH
-----------------

The player begins manufacturing and recovering:

- Telegraph keys
- Electromagnets
- Insulated copper wire
- Relay stations
- Telegraph poles

First physical communication network:

    Jakarta
       |
    Tangerang
       |
    Serang

Major gameplay shift:

Communication infrastructure now physically occupies the map.

Major conflict:

    THE COPPER WAR

Copper becomes extremely valuable.

Bandits begin stealing:
- Telegraph wire
- Electrical components
- Batteries
- Transformers

The player must protect infrastructure or develop alternatives.


ERA 3 — TELEPHONE
-----------------

Technology:
- Telephone
- Switchboards
- Relays
- Exchanges
- Better power systems

Communication becomes interactive.

Settlements can directly call each other.

The network becomes more centralized and politically important.

Major conflict:

    WHO CONTROLS THE NETWORK?

Settlements begin demanding ownership of communication infrastructure.

The player must negotiate:
- Access
- Ownership
- Maintenance responsibilities
- Network standards
- Political authority


ERA 4 — ELECTRONICS
-------------------

The old world's components are running out.

The player must begin manufacturing components.

Technologies:
- Glassworking
- Vacuum tubes
- Transformers
- Oscillators
- Amplifiers
- Logic circuits
- Primitive electronic computers

The player transitions from:

    SCAVENGE TECHNOLOGY

to:

    MANUFACTURE TECHNOLOGY


ERA 5 — COMPUTING
-----------------

The first primitive computers are constructed.

They are enormous and inefficient.

Technologies:
- Binary logic
- Memory
- Digital circuits
- CPUs
- Storage
- Modems
- Digital communication

The protagonist's programming background becomes increasingly important.

The player can now automate network operations.


ERA 6 — PACKET NETWORKS
-----------------------

The player realizes that manually maintaining dedicated connections
between every city does not scale.

Instead:

    DATA → PACKETS → ROUTERS → DESTINATION

Technologies:
- Packet switching
- Addressing
- Routing
- Error correction
- Data segmentation
- Network protocols

Every router in the country runs on salvaged silicon.

The dies themselves are fine.

Chips survive burial. Ceramic and plastic packages keep working after
decades in a drawer.

What died around them:
- Electrolytic capacitors
- Batteries
- Disks
- Screens

And the programs.

Unpowered memory does not hold its contents. Boards come back intact
and blank.

The machines survived.

The software did not.


THE DRAWER RUNS OUT
-------------------

This is the era salvage ends.

Every recovery is smaller than the last.

The network is now large enough that it consumes recovered parts
faster than the crew can find them.

Salvaged silicon is a thousand times better than anything the
workshop can build, and there is a fixed amount of it left in the
world.


THE FAB
-------

The project begins trying to manufacture its own semiconductors.

It fails.

It fails repeatedly, and each failure names something the
civilization did not know it was missing:

    Silicon purity
    Crystal growth
    Mask making
    Dopant handling
    Particulate control

None of these are workshop problems.

Each one needs a chemical industry.

A chemical industry needs a supply chain.

A supply chain needs coordination between regions that have never
worked together.

The fab is not a machine to be built.

It is the final proof that the network works.

Yield is the number the player watches:

    0 dies in 400
    1 die in 400
    3 dies in 400

Against it, salvage falls.

The crossing point is the end of the game.


ERA 7 — THE INTERNET
--------------------

The final technological breakthrough is not a machine.

It is a protocol.

Different networks can communicate with each other.

The player develops a TCP/IP-like architecture:

    NETWORK A
        |
        |
    [ROUTER]
        |
    [INTERNET]
        |
    [ROUTER]
        |
    NETWORK B

The fab succeeds here, and not before.

Its first product is a one-bit machine.

Anything pulled out of a dead appliance would embarrass it.

It is the one that matters, because tomorrow they can make another.


WHAT THE FAB IS FOR
-------------------

It is not speed.

Internetworking needs routers at every network boundary, owned by
people the player does not control.

Fifteen provinces cannot be supplied out of a drawer.

A network running on rationed scrap belongs to whoever holds the
rations.

A network that can be sold chips is a peer.

The fab is how the project stops being necessary.

It is also the answer to the question Era 3 asked:

    "Who owns communication?"

    "Not us. Not any more."


THE FINAL ACT IS DISTRIBUTION
-----------------------------

The player spends it handing gateways to networks they will never
administer, and watching autonomy spread outward from New Batavia.

Scale then forces the protocol.

Once there are enough routers in enough hands, nobody can maintain the
route tables by hand, and the network has to be allowed to decide for
itself.

The final objective:

    Send a message from New Batavia to Surabaya
    without manually specifying the route.

It travels through a gateway New Batavia manufactured, sitting in a
network New Batavia does not own, along a path nobody chose.

The protagonist tries to reconstruct the route.

He cannot.

That is the win condition.

In Era 1 he keyed the first message himself.

He was the sender, he knew the path, he was the path.

SUCCESS:

    THE FIRST INTERNET


LORE
====

THE FALL
--------

The war began in 2027.

It did not end civilization.

It only started the cascade.

The war took the things that were hardest to replace and easiest to
reach:

- Satellites
- Data centres
- Undersea cables
- Refineries
- Ports

Everything after that failed on its own.

Power grids failed because nobody could deliver fuel.

Supply chains collapsed because nobody could route them.

Telecommunications stopped because the switching was gone and the
people who understood it had scattered.

Governments lost the ability to coordinate, which meant they lost the
ability to govern.

Cities became isolated.

Modern civilization slowly fragmented.

The fighting was over in eighteen months.

The collapse took another six years, and almost nobody noticed it
happening, because it happened one unreplaced part at a time.

Jakarta survived, but became an isolated collection of settlements.

The survivors eventually began calling it:

    NEW BATAVIA


THE PROTAGONIST
===============

A telecommunications graduate who became a programmer.

During university they studied:
- Telecommunications
- Networking
- Digital communications
- Electronics
- Signal processing

After graduation they became a programmer instead.

Their knowledge is broad but incomplete.

They know what a modern network looks like.

They understand:
- Protocols
- Networking
- Servers
- Data
- Software

But they cannot personally:
- Manufacture vacuum tubes
- Build generators
- Smelt copper
- Make glass
- Design industrial machinery

This forces them to find other people.


THE RECONNECT PROJECT
=====================

The protagonist proposes an absurd idea:

    REBUILD THE INTERNET.

Most survivors think this is pointless.

Food is scarce.

Electricity is scarce.

Bandits control roads.

Why waste resources on communication?

The protagonist argues:

    "If we can communicate, we can coordinate."

    "If we can coordinate, we can trade."

    "If we can trade, we can specialize."

    "If we can specialize, we can rebuild."

The project begins with a single radio.


THE HARSH MENTOR
================

A Dr. Xeno-like character.

Former telecommunications engineer / researcher.

He is not an antagonist.

He is a harsh mentor.

He possesses deep knowledge of:
- RF engineering
- Antennas
- Transmission systems
- Electronics
- Power systems
- Industrial telecommunications
- Network reliability

His philosophy:

    "A machine that works for five minutes isn't working."

He constantly challenges the protagonist.

PROTAGONIST:
    "The transmitter works."

MENTOR:
    "Today."

PROTAGONIST:
    "What's wrong with it?"

MENTOR:
    "What happens when the generator fails?"

The mentor forces the player to think about:
- Reliability
- Redundancy
- Maintenance
- Safety
- Scalability

He believes civilization must be rebuilt correctly.

The protagonist believes civilization must be rebuilt quickly.

Their philosophies gradually converge.


MENTOR CHARACTER ARC
====================

Early:

    Engineering > People

He believes people are unreliable.

Middle:

    Technology exists because of people.

He begins teaching rather than simply criticizing.

Late:

    Engineering + People

He accepts that civilization cannot be optimized like a machine.

His final philosophy becomes:

    "Build it so everyone can use it.
     Then build it so it survives them."

CHARACTER ROSTER
================

The archetypes above, named and dated.

Nobody on this list is a stat bonus.

Each one gates a specific technology, and the era does not open until
that person exists.


THE FOUNDERS
------------

BAYU — Architect
    Telecommunications graduate who became a programmer.
    The protagonist.
    Joins: Era 0. Without him nothing starts.

MEL — Quartermaster
    Container driver out of Tanjung Priok, six years.
    Knows which roads are open and which are watched.
    Joins: Era 0. Without her nothing moves.

PAK MIN — Wireman
    Thirty-one years at the Gambir exchange.
    The harsh mentor.
    Joins: Era 1. Without him no transmitter works twice.
    Dies: Era 5, of being old, in a workshop that works.

TAJUDDIN — Warden
    Stands between the copper and the people who want it.
    Joins: Era 1. Without him the copper walks off.

The founders persist for the whole game.

They are the emotional through-line, and they are what the skits are
made of.

What changes is what they are: labour in the metro eras, programs at
island scale, chairs and founders at archipelago scale.

By Era 7 the four of them command nothing directly.

That is the arc, not a limitation.


THE BUILDERS
------------

IWAN — Machinist
    Motorcycle mechanic.
    Engines, generators, pole hardware, improvisation.
    Joins: Era 2. Without him there is no power and nothing to bolt to.

SARI — Lineman
    Climbs, strings, splices.
    Joins: Era 2. Without her a hundred kilometres of wire never leaves
    the drum.


THE INSTITUTION
---------------

AYU — Switchman
    The first operator Bayu ever trained.
    Runs the exchanges and trains the people who run the rest.
    Joins: Era 3. Without her the switchboards have nobody at them.

IBU RATNA — Envoy
    Speaks for the settlements the network now runs through.
    Joins: Era 3. Without her Bandung takes its exchange back.


THE MANUFACTURERS
-----------------

HENDRA — Glassblower
    Joins: Era 4. Without him there are no vacuum tubes, ever.

DEWI — Chemist
    Batteries, acids, solvents — and, much later, dopants.
    Joins: Era 4. Without her the fab is not even a possibility.


THE SUCCESSORS
--------------

FAJAR — Coder
    Born after the fall.

    The first person on the project who never saw the thing they are
    rebuilding.

    Everyone else is working from memory. He is working from
    description.

    This makes him the only one who can design without nostalgia, and
    the only one who cannot be argued with using "that is not how it
    used to work".

    Joins: Era 5. Without him Bayu stays the only programmer alive.

ANISA — Mathematician
    Error correction, addressing, routing.
    Joins: Era 6. Without her packets have nowhere to go.


PAK MIN DIES IN ERA 5
---------------------

Not to a raid. Not to a fall from a mast.

He is old, and the project has run for twenty years, and one morning
he does not come to the bench.

What matters is what he knew when he died.

He knew, in detail, how a semiconductor is made.

He could describe the furnace. He could describe the crystal. He had
read every page of it, decades before the war, and he could recite the
process to anyone who asked.

He never saw one made.

Not because the knowledge was lost — it was in his hands the entire
time — but because knowing how a thing is done and being able to do it
are separated by a chemical industry, a supply chain, and a generation
of people who have to be trained by someone.

    Knowledge survives a collapse easily.

    Capability does not.

    Rebuilding capability is the work, and the work takes a lifetime.

This is the correction to the fantasy the genre usually sells.

The protagonist does not read a book and produce a transistor.

Nobody does.

The project takes forty years because forty years is what it takes,
and the man who understood it best is not alive at the end of them.

His last instruction to Fajar is not a technique.

    "Write it down properly. With the date."

That is the same thing he said in Era 1, about a circuit that answered
twice.

The founders' philosophy, complete:

    People die.

    Ideas do not.

    But an idea nobody can act on is just a rumour with footnotes,
    and closing that gap is the only thing any of this has ever been.


ERA 7 RECRUITS NOBODY
---------------------

The last people the player meets are not recruits.

They are counterparts: network founders in Surabaya, Medan, Makassar,
running networks the player does not administer and cannot order.

That is the point.

The ending is the project becoming unnecessary, and the roster stops
growing because the work has stopped being theirs.

======================

----------------

    BANDITS

    Scrap, food, information

    Radio towers are attacked and generators stolen.

-----------------

    COPPER THEFT

-----------------

    POLITICAL CONTROL

    "Who owns communication?"

-------------------
Main threat:
    RESOURCE SCARCITY

    Who controls the Internet?


FACTIONS
========

THE RECONNECT MOVEMENT
----------------------

Goal:
    Build an open, resilient communication network.

Philosophy:
    Communication is a basic infrastructure of civilization.


THE SCRAP RAIDERS
-----------------

Goal:
    Immediate survival.

Philosophy:

    "Tomorrow doesn't matter if we don't survive today."

They dismantle infrastructure for resources.


THE INDUSTRIAL LEAGUE
---------------------

A collection of powerful settlements and factories.

They believe infrastructure should belong to whoever builds it.

They want to charge settlements for network access.


THE CENTRAL AUTHORITY
---------------------

A growing political government.

They believe a unified country requires a unified communication system.

They want centralized control of the network.


THE FREE NETWORK MOVEMENT
-------------------------

A later faction.

They believe communication should be open to everyone.

They become allies of the protagonist but may also create problems by
allowing uncontrolled access.


GAME THEMES
===========

The game explores:

    Technology vs survival

    Centralization vs decentralization

    Knowledge vs control

    Short-term survival vs long-term civilization

    Infrastructure vs politics

    Efficiency vs resilience

    Individual expertise vs collective knowledge

    Knowing how vs being able to


THE DR. STONE FEELING
=====================

The player should constantly experience:

    "We need X."

    "Where do we get X?"

    "Nobody knows how to make X anymore."

    "Find someone who knows."

Then:

    EXPLORE
       ↓
    FIND PERSON
       ↓
    LEARN KNOWLEDGE
       ↓
    GATHER MATERIALS
       ↓
    RECREATE TECHNOLOGY
       ↓
    SOLVE NEW PROBLEM

The protagonist isn't a godlike genius who knows everything.

They are the person who understands:

    "We need to figure this out."

The real civilization is rebuilt by hundreds of specialists.

FINAL SCENE
===========

New Batavia.

The player has connected Jakarta, Bandung, Semarang, Yogyakarta,
Surabaya, and eventually settlements across the Indonesian archipelago.

The final packet is sent.

Destination:

    SURABAYA

Source:

    NEW BATAVIA

The player doesn't choose the route.

The network does.

The packet travels through several independent networks.

Then:

    MESSAGE RECEIVED

The entire team watches the terminal.

Silence.

The protagonist says:

    "It's back."

The harsh mentor looks at the network map.

    "No."

The protagonist looks confused.

    "We didn't bring it back."

He smiles.

    "We built a new one."
