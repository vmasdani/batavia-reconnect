WRITING THE INDONESIAN
======================

How the two scripts relate, what the Indonesian is allowed to do, and the
specific ways a good English line turns into a bad Indonesian one.

Written after four passes over Era 0 and Era 1, most of which found the
same handful of faults again. It exists so the fifth pass does not have
to rediscover them.

Companion to spec.txt. The rules here are enforced partly by
tools/script-check.ts and mostly by reading.


THE CONTRACT
============

The English files own the structure. Which era, who speaks, how many
lines, where the scenes break — all of it lives in story.ts, skits.ts
and era0-skits.ts, and none of it is repeated in the Indonesian.

The Indonesian files supply text at matching indices, and nothing else:

    story.ts        <-  story.id.ts
    era0-skits.ts   <-  era0-skits.id.ts
    skits.ts        <-  skits.id.ts

A translation therefore cannot drift out of step with the scene it
belongs to. The worst that can happen is a missing line, which falls
back to English rather than rendering blank, and a line-count mismatch,
which check:script reports.

WHAT THE INDONESIAN MAY CHANGE
------------------------------

Wording, rhythm, imagery, sentence count within one line, and any figure
of speech that does not survive the crossing. It is a performance of the
scene, not a gloss of the sentence.

WHAT IT MAY NOT CHANGE
----------------------

Facts, numbers, names, and any motif the rest of the script leans on.

This is the failure that costs the most and shows the least. Two real
examples, both found long after they were introduced:

    EN    It used to take 8 seconds to call them.
    BAD   Dulu kalo pake telpon kan ga sampe beberapa detik.
    GOOD  Dulu buat nelpon mereka cuma 8 detik.

8 seconds is the spine of the whole script. There is an Era 0 skit
called "8 detik", Pak Min says it again in Era 1, and the relay card
times the same errand nine times against it. The vague translation meant
an Indonesian player never heard the number in the scene that introduces
it, so two later scenes lost their echo.

    EN    I think the problem is that we have 3 days of rice.
          Your map does not show that.
    BAD   Dari perspektif lu, itu lu lagi baca peta. Gua ngitung jumlah
          gudang yang ada. Itu pekerjaan yang beda.
    GOOD  Masalahnya beras kita tinggal 3 hari. Itu ga keliatan di peta
          lu.

The invented version dropped the rice, and four lines later Bayu replies
to an argument nobody made in Indonesian.

    EN    And you think that is the problem.
    BAD   Nah, berarti lu ngerti masalahnya.
    GOOD  Terus menurut lu itu masalahnya?

Bayu is challenging Mel. The bad line has him agreeing with her, which
turns her correction in the next line into an addition and takes the
argument out of the scene.


WHO SPEAKS HOW
==============

Two registers, and they are not per-character. They are per-pair.

THE FOUNDERS - gua, lu, no ceremony, elder or not
    bayu  mel  tajuddin  pakmin

    They walked the region together and never stopped working together.
    Pak Min is thirty years older than Bayu and still says lu. Distance
    shows up as clipping a name (Bay), not as a pronoun.

EVERYONE WHO JOINED LATER - saya, and the founders by title
    iwan  sari  ayu  ratna  hendra  dewi  fajar  anisa

    The project is their work but the founders' whole life. Neither
    slang nor written Indonesian: ordinary polite speech.

THE NARRATOR - written Indonesian
    tidak, sudah, kau, mengucapkannya. The only voice that gets them.

THE ONE ASYMMETRY THAT MATTERS
------------------------------

Bayu says saya and Bapak to Pak Min, and gua and lu to Mel and
Tajuddin, in the same scene. Era 1's opening establishes it at their
first meeting and it never goes away.

A skit that has Bayu saying lu to Pak Min is wrong even though both men
are in the founders' list. check:script cannot catch this, because it
knows who is speaking and not who is being spoken to.

NUMBERS
-------

2 and above are digits, in both languages. People say "3 hari", not
"tiga hari". One stays a word: it usually means "a single", not a count.

Ordinals, fractions and dua-duanya are exempt. check:script enforces
this and maintains the exemption list.


THE SIX RULES
=============

English survives a word-for-word translation. Indonesian does not. Every
one of these is a fault found in shipped lines, not a style preference.

1. A CONTRAST NAMES ITS SECOND TERM
-----------------------------------

English can say "that is different" and leave the comparison hanging.
Indonesian says what it is different from.

    EN    No. They were deciding whether to let us through.
    BAD   Engga. Mereka lagi mikir. Itu beda, dan lu bakal cepet belajar
          bedainnya.
    GOOD  Engga. Mereka lagi mikir, mau ngelewatin kita apa engga. Beda
          sama takut. Lama-lama lu bisa bedain kok.

The same rule covers verbs whose object English drops:

    EN    Dogs, mostly. They know before we do.
    BAD   Mereka tau duluan sebelum kita tau.
    GOOD  Mereka tau duluan kalo ada perampok, jauh sebelum kita tau.

    EN    It is the only one I cannot see.
    BAD   Cuma itu yang ga keliatan sama gua.
    GOOD  Cuma itu yang ga keliatan dari sini, jadi gua ga tau ada yang
          ngeliatin kita apa engga.

2. ABSTRACT NOUN PREDICATES BECOME VERBS
----------------------------------------

"That was the whole negotiation" is an essay sentence in either
language. Indonesian says the action.

    EN    That was enough.
    BAD   Itu seluruh negosiasinya.
    GOOD  Udah, segitu doang.

The medical-report register is the same fault wearing a different coat:

    EN    before my knees get worse
    BAD   sebelum dengkul gua tambah parah
    GOOD  sebelum encok gua kambuh

"tambah parah" is a calque of "get worse". Indonesian has a fixed
collocation for a body complaint - kambuh - and it pairs with encok.

3. SENTENCE-FINAL PARTICLES CARRY STANCE
----------------------------------------

    kok  deh  sih  aja  dong  tuh  nih  lah  mah  ya

Speech without them reads as translated. The first draft of Era 0's
skits had 4 particle tokens across 51 lines; the revision has 18.

They are placed, not sprinkled. A particle goes where somebody actually
has an attitude. The heavy scenes stay deliberately bare - eight-seconds
and most of letter-waiting carry almost none, and that is what makes
them land differently from the rest.

4. CLAUSES ARE JOINED OUT LOUD
------------------------------

English leaves adjacent full stops and lets the reader infer the join.
Indonesian says it.

    terus  padahal  makanya  giliran  soalnya  ujung-ujungnya  emang

    EN    Nobody notices a thing while it works. Then it is gone, and
          you spend the rest of your life paying attention.
    GOOD  Selama barangnya masih jalan, emang ga ada yang merhatiin.
          Giliran udah ilang, baru deh lu merhatiin seumur idup.

5. A TOPIC PIVOT NEEDS A SIGNAL
-------------------------------

English can change subject on a beat. Indonesian names what prompted
the change, or the new line reads as a non sequitur.

    EN    What do you think about while you walk?
    BAD   Lu mikirin apa sih pas lagi jalan?
    GOOD  Ngomong-ngomong, dari tadi gua perhatiin lu kayak ga tenang.
          Lagi mikirin apa sih?

    EN    How far is Bogor?
    BAD   Berapa jauh Bogor?
    GOOD  Bentar. Gua tes omongan lu. Bogor berapa jauh dari sini?

Tajuddin is testing the claim Bayu made one line earlier. The English
question carries that; the bare Indonesian question does not.

A war story, a piece of biography or an example arriving with no
handover is the same fault. Gua ngerti, Nah contohnya gini, Ya udah -
one clause is usually enough.

6. USE IMAGES THAT EXIST HERE
-----------------------------

A figure of speech is not translated. It is replaced by one that carries
the same weight for a reader who grew up here.

    EN    A crude radio can answer once.
    BAD   Jari basah sama silet juga bisa jawab sekali.
    GOOD  Kabel kesenggol doang juga bisa bikin dia jawab sekali.

    A wet finger and a razor blade is a foxhole crystal set. The
    reference does not exist here, so what survives is a literal wet
    finger.

    EN    You learn to be the envelope. It is easier than being the
          letter.
    BAD   Lama-lama lu belajar jadi amplopnya.
    GOOD  Lama-lama lu belajar jadi yang nganterin doang, bukan yang
          dianterin.

    English personifies stationery. Indonesian reads it literally.

    EN    before you put your boots on
    BAD   sebelum lu kelar pake sepatu
    GOOD  sebelum lu sempet berdiri

    An English idiom for "before you can react", carrying footwear
    nobody here wears.

    EN    I still liked being needed.
          Then get used to being useful instead.
    BAD   ...jadi orang yang dibutuhin. / ...jadi orang yang kepake aja.
    GOOD  ...jadi orang yang dibutuhin. / ...jadi salah satu, bukan
          satu-satunya.

    English plays needed against useful. In Indonesian dibutuhin and
    kepake are near-synonyms, so the retort collapses into repetition.
    The distinction that does exist here is being one of many against
    being the only one.

    EN    Every one of those racks was a street.
    BAD   Tiap rak di situ itu satu jalan.
    GOOD  Tiap rak di situ satu nama jalan.

    English metonymy. In Indonesian "satu jalan" also reads as "one
    way", so the sentence is ambiguous until the street names arrive.

Sometimes the swap improves on the English. "Then plant the radio tower,
boy" is a deliberate pun in English; nanem tiang is ordinary Indonesian
for erecting a mast, so the joke lands harder here than there. Keep
those.


ONE WORD PER THING
==================

A term the player will meet again keeps the same Indonesian word
everywhere, across all six script files.

    circuit, the end-to-end link      jalur
    raiders, the thing that takes it  perampok
    the era 1 transmit control        tombol pancar
    the era 2 telegraph key           kunci
    courier                           kurir

Three of these were found as inconsistencies, not chosen in advance:

- circuit was jalur in the story and rangkaian in the skits. rangkaian
  is a circuit of components, which is a different thing from a 100 km
  link.
- the Era 1 skit called the transmit control a telegraph key. Era 1 is
  AM voice radio; the key belongs to Era 2, and the storyline already
  had tombol pancar.
- maling was considered for the night-time threat and rejected: the game
  calls it perampokan in the briefings, and maling is a sneak thief
  rather than a group that can carry off a generator.

The glossary is the tiebreaker. If a term has an Indonesian name or
alias in glossary.id.ts, the script uses that spelling, because that is
what makes it light up for the reader.


HOW TO AUDIT A PASS
===================

check:script catches four things and nothing else: line counts, spelled
numbers, glossary coverage, diagram pin counts, and the pronoun register
of whoever is speaking. Everything in THE SIX RULES is invisible to it.

So the audit is done by reading, with two throwaway scripts that make
reading cheap. Both live in the scratchpad, not in the repo.

1. DUMP THE PAIRS

Print every spoken line as EN above ID, with speaker and index, skipping
the narrator. Reading English and Indonesian side by side is what
surfaces drift; neither file alone shows it.

Filter to the eras being worked on. A whole-script dump is too long to
read carefully, and a pass that is not read carefully finds nothing.

2. COMPARE GLOSSARY LIGHTING

For the same eras, collect which terms findTerms lights in the English
text and which in the Indonesian, and diff the two sets.

A term that lights in one language and not the other is either a
translation that lost a technology word, or an alias list with a hole in
it. This check found both, plus two defects in the English:

- "Key it again" in an Era 1 skit lit telegraph-key, a term Era 2
  introduces. Fixed to "Press it again".
- "envelope" was an alias of glassblowing, for a valve's glass envelope.
  It lit on Era 0's line about a letter. Alias removed.

Neither would ever have been noticed by reading the English alone.

3. COUNT PARTICLES

A per-file tally of the ten particles in rule 3. Not a target to hit -
it is a smell test. Four in fifty lines means the pass has not started.


WHAT THIS DOCUMENT IS NOT
=========================

It is not a style guide for the English. The English is written first
and on its own terms; when it is wrong, it gets fixed as English.

It is not a licence to rewrite. Every example above changed a line
because the Indonesian was failing at something specific and nameable.
A line that reads well and says what the English says is finished, and
the fact that it could be phrased differently is not a reason.
