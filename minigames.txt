MINIGAMES
=========

Notes, not decisions. One per era, each built around the choice the people who
actually did this had to make. Eras 0 and 1 are built; the rest are not.

Two rules that apply to all of them
-----------------------------------

1. Skippable, with an honest auto-resolve. These get replayed. A player who has
   proved they can key Morse should be able to hand it to a clerk at a fair
   rate rather than key every message for the rest of the era.

2. Each one has to BE the era's real decision, not a reflex test in the era's
   costume. Where a minigame cannot be that, the era is better off without one.


ERA 0 — THE PARLEY                                                       [BUILT]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The problem was era0.ts:736: a single roll.

    win = 0.34 + rations/40 * 0.28 + classBonus + attempts * 0.07

Every decision the player made happened before the walk. The era's central
action — talking a village into the network — was the one thing the player did
not do.

What is there now, in `parley.ts` and `Parley.tsx`: **one line and one click.**

  - The village says what it needs. Food, somewhere to leave a message, word
    carried to kin in another town, or a watch on the ridge.
  - Four offers, one for each: rations, a board on your gate, a fire on your
    hill, we carry your word. They are the era's own economy, so choosing one
    teaches boards and fires rather than teaching a minigame.
  - Right offer, they are in. Wrong offer, they want a price — a second visit,
    not a closed door, and by then the player knows the answer.
  - `attempts` stayed as memory. The dice went.

A place needs what it needs, dealt round-robin down a hash ordering so the four
wants come out even across thirteen settlements. A settlement whose hill sees
nobody never wants a watch, because the roster already marks that hill blind and
a right answer the map calls impossible reads as the game lying.

The rations offer takes 4 out of the store, or whatever is in it when there is
less. It is never off the table: an earlier build blocked it below 4, and since
the larder sits near empty through the first fortnight, that made a village that
needed food unwinnable — a headless run stalled at 3 of 13 settlements on day 11
and never moved again. What can be given is a price, not a gate.


Why it is this small
--------------------

The first build was much bigger: a hidden **fear** as well as a hidden want,
three rounds, six offers, a running score, four reply tiers. It read well and it
was the wrong thing to build. The player arrives at this screen in the middle of
planning who walks where and deciding where the wood goes — it is a stop on the
way through a day, not a game to sit down with. Two riddles and an arithmetic
budget on top of route planning is a puzzle inside a puzzle.

Cut, and worth naming so it is not re-invented:

  - the fear axis, and with it the "nothing, and say so" offer that answered
    being owed, and Tajuddin waiting outside — a genuine tradeoff (he wins the
    village that fears raiders and loses the one that fears being occupied) and
    much better writing than the flat -0.08 penalty it replaced. It cost a
    whole second hidden variable to run.
  - two of the three rounds, the score, and the speaker bonuses.

If any of it comes back it should be as flavour on the reply, not as a second
thing to solve.


Three things the first draft of this note got wrong, corrected in the build
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - **Rations were never spent.** `offer = min(1, rations/40)` only *read* the
    larder.

  - **The old offer term fired at the wrong time.** OFFER_FULL and RATION_CAP
    were both 40, while income is 3 + 0.5 per talking settlement against 3 crew
    eating. The larder is near-empty during the first parleys and full when
    there is nothing left to win. The whole term is gone.

  - **Parleys resolve in a batch.** `resolveSurveyDay` loops the crew, so up to
    3 exchanges can land on one end-day click. The day is now split: `era0.ts`
    reports who is sitting down (`parleySeats`), the store queues them one at a
    time, and `resolveSurveyDay` takes the outcomes as an argument. A seat with
    no outcome is played deaf by the same rules, which is what makes the skip
    button honest and what keeps `tools/survey-balance.ts` running headless.

Volume is bounded, which is why any of this is affordable: contact never decays,
and the win needs 8 of 13 settlements, so a run is roughly 10 exchanges.


ERA 1 — THE TUNING BENCH                                                 [BUILT]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Where this note has been: the first draft wanted a nightly board with power,
mast height and a time slot on it. None of those three exist. Mast height is a
fixed field per settlement in `world.ts`, `link.nightOnly` is derived from
geometry inside `refreshLinks` with no notion of time of day, and there is no
transmit-power variable anywhere in the model. RAID_PER_EXPOSED_SITE counts
*unguarded* sites, not loud ones.

What was built: a frequency bench, run once per site, at the moment the set goes
on the air. `commission` gives it power, an operator and stage 3 — tuning
belongs there, because there is nothing to tune until there is power in it, and
because it is a moment the player already stops at 13 times a run.

The bench is one gesture. A dial from 1600 to 7400 kHz with a dead gap in the
middle, one carrier hiding in each half, and a meter. Drag until the meter
climbs, stop on the peak, lock it in. Which half you stopped in is the site's
**band**; how near the peak you got is its **trim**.

    src/tuning.ts    the dial, the carriers, the score, the clerk
    src/Tuning.tsx   the bench and the picture
    src/sim.ts       `carry`, `bandsOf`, band-aware `refreshLinks`, `tuningSeats`

The picture is why it is worth stopping for. A strip measured in multiples of
the circuit's own reach — so one drawing is true for every neighbour whatever
its mast height — with the zones shaded and this site's circuits marked on it.
Slide across the gap and the shading changes under them: the sky wave's skip
zone becomes a hole you watch a settlement fall into. That is the one place in
the game where propagation is a thing you can see rather than a glossary entry.

    ground   0 .. 1 reach daylight, out to 1.65 after dark
    sky      0 .. 0.35 close in, DEAD to 0.9, then out to 2.6 after dark
             at full speed, because what arrives is a strong signal

A link needs both ends on the same band. A **hardened** site holds both, which
gives `harden` a job beyond slowing wear and makes a relay station mean what a
relay station means. Off channel is its own link status — the fix is a bench,
not a kit, so it should not read as "silent".

Trim runs 0.6 to 1 and multiplies the circuit's margin. Floored well above zero
on purpose: a botched tune costs speed on everything the site carries and never
takes it off the air. A minigame that can brick a site is one players stop
trusting.


What the numbers said, and what that meant for the design
---------------------------------------------------------

Measured before any of it was written. The region is 25 km across and the masts
are tall, so every link but one sits far inside daylight reach:

    batavia-tangerang       20.8 km   reach 49.4
    tangerang-serang        53.5 km   reach 50.9   night only, half speed
    the other ten            3.2 to 21.5 km, all reach 39 to 97

So the **band** changes exactly 2 of the 12 links: Batavia-Tangerang dies on the
sky wave (20.8 km lands in a 17.3-44.4 km hole) and Tangerang-Serang goes from
4 wpm at night to full speed. That is not a network-wide decision, it is a
Serang decision, and the era's new objective falls straight out of it: harden
Tangerang so it can hold the ground wave for Batavia and the sky wave for Serang
at once. Measured: 17 wpm and 9 wpm (night), against `off channel` if Tangerang
is not hardened.

The **trim** is what runs at all 13 commissions, and it is the half that earns
its keep on a map this small.

Every seed site is `band: 'ground'`, `trim: 1`, so the authored opening is
byte-identical to what it was — diffed against the pre-change build, all twelve
links unchanged.


What was left out
-----------------

  - **Trim does not decay.** It is set once and multiplies margin from then on.
    Tying it to `wear` would double-count what `worn` already does, and giving
    `repair` a second bench was more machinery than the idea is worth.
  - **Co-channel interference**, from the last draft of this note. It is the
    true reason frequency assignment is a job, and it would make the band a
    decision at every site rather than at two — but all thirteen sites start on
    one channel, so it needs its own balance pass, not a paragraph.
  - **A bigger map.** The band is a two-site decision because the region is
    25 km across. Somewhere past Serang would change that, and it is a larger
    piece of work than any minigame in this file.


ERA 2 — THE KEY
---------------

Tap a word. Hold for a dash. The receiving clerk's skill decides how sloppy the
player is allowed to be, which ties straight to MAX_WPM.

Teaches the thing the glossary entry says and nobody believes until they try it:
the silences are as much of the code as the marks.


ERA 3 — THE SWITCHBOARD
-----------------------

Cords, jacks, a queue. Calls arrive wanting a number; patch, hold, tear down.
Rush hour brings more calls than there are cords.

The era's win condition is automating yourself out of the minigame, which is the
best joke available in this whole game and is also exactly what happened to Ayu.


ERA 4 — THE GLASS BENCH
-----------------------

Shape a tube: heat, blow, seal, pump, test. Too little heat and it will not
move; too much and it collapses.

Yield-based rather than pass/fail — 14 attempts, some crack. Teaches that the
bottleneck of the era is craft, not theory, which is the whole argument Hendra
makes in the opening.


ERA 5 — THE CORE PLANE
----------------------

Either: thread the rings on a wire grid to a pattern, half-current on each wire
so only the crossing flips.

Or: the routing-table drill. Here is a table and a packet — pick the port. The
wrong port and the player watches the packet loop. Teaches the thing that is
hard to say in a sentence: the node does not know the route.


ERA 6 — THE SORTER
------------------

A message split into numbered packets. Lines go up and down mid-flight. Get them
all through and reassembled. Noise flips a bit, the checksum catches it, the far
end asks for that one again.

Store and forward plus error correction, played rather than read.


ERA 7 — THE HANDSHAKE
---------------------

Two networks that agree on nothing. Map the envelope fields so the gateway can
carry one into the other without understanding either.

Or the harder version: set your own timeout and acknowledgement rules, then
watch a lossy link and find out whether what you just designed deadlocks.


BUILD ORDER
-----------

Era 0 is built. It was moved to the front of the queue because it was the
biggest real complaint — the era's central action was a dice roll — and because
era 1's original plan turned out to need model changes that era 0's did not.

Era 1 is built — the trim and the band together, because the trim alone is a
skill check with nothing to decide and the band alone would only ever fire at
Tangerang.

Era 2's key next. It is the smallest of the rest and it is the one the glossary
already sets up: the silences are as much of the code as the marks.
