INSPIRATIONS
============

This game was not designed from a design document. It accumulated.
It is the residue of years of playing games, watching anime, and living
inside pop culture — things that stuck, got chewed on for a long time,
and eventually surfaced as a single idea about rebuilding a network in a
dead Indonesia.

What follows is the honest list of where each piece came from.


DIRECT INSPIRATIONS
===================

CONCEPT — Dr. Stone
-------------------
The spine of the whole thing. Civilization is gone, and one person with
an education rebuilds it from first principles, in order, each invention
standing on the last. The joy is not in the fighting; it is in the tech
tree being *earned* — you cannot have a radio before you have copper
wire, and you cannot have copper wire before you have someone willing to
dig. Dr. Stone also gave permission for the tone: rebuilding is allowed
to be exciting rather than grim.

APOCALYPSE & THE CONSEQUENCES OF WAR — Fallout
----------------------------------------------
The texture of the ruined world. Not just "everything is broken" but
what people *did* with the broken pieces: scavenger economies, settlements
with their own politics, infrastructure repurposed by people who do not
understand it. Fallout is also where the Copper War comes from — the idea
that a resource becomes a reason for violence, and that the old world's
leftovers are a finite, contested inheritance.

ISOLATED CIVILIZATION TRYING TO RECONNECT — Death Stranding
-----------------------------------------------------------
The emotional thesis. Connection as the actual objective, not a means to
one. Every settlement joined to the network is a real gain, and the map
slowly filling with links is the reward. Death Stranding also proved that
logistics and infrastructure can carry a story — that the act of laying a
line between two places is meaningful on its own.

TIMESPAN BETWEEN YEARS — Like a Dragon: Stranger Than Heaven (RGG)
------------------------------------------------------------------
The structure of jumping across years, with the world and the cast
visibly aged between chapters. The project takes forty years because
forty years is what it takes. Characters get old, retire, die, and hand
their work to someone younger. The time skip is not a narrative shortcut;
it is the point.

SKIT MODE — Tales of series
---------------------------
Short, optional, character-driven conversations that trigger off world
state. They carry almost none of the plot and almost all of the warmth.
The specialists are the emotional through-line, and skits are how the
player actually gets to know them — the radio operator complaining about
interference, the exchange veteran explaining how it used to work.

"DISPATCHING" GAMEPLAY — Dispatch, 911 Operator
------------------------------------------------
The moment-to-moment texture of running the network. You are not out
there doing it; you are the one receiving the call, deciding who goes,
and living with the fact that you only had two teams and three problems.
Triage under incomplete information is a better fit for a communications
game than direct control ever would be.

PIPELINE BUILDING & CRAFTING — Factorio
----------------------------------------
The systems layer. Throughput, bottlenecks, intermediate components,
production chains that have to be planned backwards from what you want.
Factorio is why the network has bandwidth and latency and not just a
"connected / not connected" flag — the link is a *pipe*, and pipes can be
saturated, starved, and rerouted.

"INFORMATION IS KEY" — Metal Gear Solid
----------------------------------------
The philosophy underneath everything. MGS2 in particular: who controls
the flow of information controls what people believe, and a network is
never politically neutral. This is where the later eras get their
conflicts — WHO CONTROLS THE NETWORK is an MGS question. Also the codec:
disembodied voices over a radio link, delivering exposition, advice, and
personality, are the oldest form of connection in games and still the
warmest.


OTHER GAMES THIS COULD DRAW FROM
================================

Possible additional inspirations, grouped by what they would contribute.

NETWORK AS A LIVING, FRAGILE SYSTEM
-----------------------------------
- Mini Metro — the purest expression of "you are designing a topology,
  not placing buildings." Watching a clean network degrade into spaghetti
  as demand grows is exactly the failure mode the player should feel.
- Opus Magnum / Zachtronics generally — the solution-space feeling: many
  valid topologies, scored on cost, latency, and reliability, with the
  player quietly competing against their own earlier designs.
- Transport Tycoon / OpenTTD — routing, congestion, and the long-term
  consequences of a line you laid twenty years ago and never revisited.
- Uplink / Hacknet — the network seen from the inside, as something you
  navigate rather than something you look down on. Useful for the later
  packet-switching eras.

REBUILDING AND SETTLEMENT MANAGEMENT
------------------------------------
- Frostpunk — the moral cost of keeping infrastructure alive, and laws
  that permanently change what your society is. Directly applicable to
  the "who controls the network" conflicts.
- Rimworld — colonists as individuals with skills, histories, and
  relationships, who can be lost. Fits the specialist system.
- Banished / Against the Storm — settlement survival where the failure is
  slow and systemic rather than a sudden defeat.
- Terra Nil — restoration as a win condition. A useful counterweight to
  Fallout's fatalism.

STORY, TIME, AND CHARACTER
--------------------------
- Disco Elysium — dialogue-heavy, politically literate, willing to let
  characters argue about the future of a broken place. A model for how
  the settlement negotiations could read.
- Pentiment — decades passing, consequences landing on the next
  generation, the same town seen at different ages.
- 80 Days — travel and communication across a mapped world, with the
  route itself as the narrative.
- Return of the Obra Dinn — reconstructing what happened from fragmentary
  evidence; good shape for the "what actually caused the collapse"
  backstory.

SIGNAL, RADIO, AND ISOLATION
----------------------------
- Firewatch — a relationship conducted entirely over a radio link.
- Signalis / Oxenfree — radio as texture, atmosphere, and interference.
- Papers, Please — bureaucratic triage under time pressure, with people
  on the other side of the desk. Close cousin to the dispatch layer.
- Beholder / This War of Mine — the human cost of decisions made from a
  desk, at a distance.

INDUSTRY AND PRODUCTION CHAINS
------------------------------
- Dyson Sphere Program — scaling production across a whole map, and the
  logistics of distance.
- Shapez — pure throughput puzzles, useful for component manufacturing.
- Anno series — production chains tied to regions with different
  resources, which fits an archipelago map well.
- Workers & Resources: Soviet Republic — infrastructure planning where
  power, transport, and labour all have to line up, and nothing works
  until all three do.

ANIME AND NON-GAME
------------------
- Ghost in the Shell — networks as a substrate for society and identity;
  the political weight of infrastructure.
- Legend of the Galactic Heroes — long timespans, generational politics,
  institutions outliving the people who founded them.
- Connections (James Burke) — the documentary logic of one invention
  making the next one possible, which is the tech tree in essay form.
- The Wire, season by season — institutions as the real protagonist.
